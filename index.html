OUR APPROACH

Accelerating youth potential through art and trust.

Over 100,000 children in Lagos waterfront slums face hazardous shoreline scrap labor and lack access to formal schools. Slum Art Foundation operates on a community-first co-investment model: we provide fine-art training, safe floating classrooms, and mentorship.

When a child paints, 100% of the proceeds from their artwork are placed in a formal education escrow that pays their primary and secondary school tuition, books, uniforms, and hot daily meals.

HOW IT WORKS

It starts with creativity.

Four simple steps that turn waste materials and raw youth talent into lifelong education, dignity, and independence.

Step 01

Safe creative haven

We welcome street-vulnerable children in Ijora Badia & Makoko into a safe studio environment away from shoreline scrap scavenging.

Step 02

Upcycling the lagoon

Repurposing 50,000+ plastic bottles into Makoko’s first solar-powered floating classroom and community learning sanctuary.

Step 03

Fine art mastery

Mentored by 4× Guinness World Record holder Adetunwase Adenle in acrylics, charcoal, mosaic sculpture, and narrative enterprise.

Step 04

100% Tuition Escrow

Sales from original artworks and comic book pledges go directly into an escrow paying 100% of school tuition, books, and daily hot meals.

OUR IMPACT

Direct, sustained progress from inside the community.

Slum Art investments deliver quick, tangible impact and self-reliance. This leads to growth in skills, trust, and community pride—advancing development and readying children for brighter futures.

FOUNDER & VISIONARY

Adetunwase Adenle

Educator, Visual Artist & 4-Time Guinness World Record Holder

Talent exists in every corner of the world, but opportunity does not. We ensure a child born in a slum never feels their dreams are capped by their environment.

In 2018, Adetunwase began clearing thousands of discarded plastic bottles choking the Lagos lagoon. Upcycling 50,000+ bottles into Makoko’s first solar-powered floating art academy, he created a sanctuary where street-vulnerable youth master fine art—using artwork sales to fund 100% of their formal primary and secondary schooling.
